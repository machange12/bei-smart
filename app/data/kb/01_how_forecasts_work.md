# How AgriPulse Forecasts Are Made

AgriPulse serves around 270 live forecast series across about 80 markets in all eight of Kenya's regions. Most are maize, beans, sorghum, millet and rice, with a few others such as groundnuts, maize flour and wheat. Each series has forecasts at three horizons: 3, 6, and 12 months. Every series is modelled on its own rather than pooled together, because a single model trained across all series learns an average that fits none of them well.

Three model families are used: Prophet, SARIMA, and Chronos-Bolt, a pre-trained foundation model that needs no fitting. For each series, all three are tested on held-out months the models never saw, and the one that performs best is selected to serve live forecasts — Chronos wins most often, on a little over half of all series. Where a blend of the two best models clearly beats the single winner, the series is served as a weighted blend instead, with the more accurate model counting for more; roughly one series in six is forecast this way. Across all served series the typical (median) test error is around 9–10%, and every forecast is refitted each month when new data arrives.

The forecasts you see are pre-computed from this pipeline, not generated on the spot. Each one carries the model that produced it, its measured test error, and a confidence label, so the number comes with a sense of how much to trust it.
