# How AgriPulse Forecasts Are Made

AgriPulse serves around 270 live forecast series across about 80 markets in all eight of Kenya's regions. Most are maize, beans, sorghum, millet and rice, with a few others such as groundnuts, maize flour and wheat. Each series has forecasts at three horizons: 3, 6, and 12 months. Every series is modelled on its own rather than pooled together, because a single model trained across all series learns an average that fits none of them well.

Four models are used: Prophet, SARIMA, Chronos-2 (a pre-trained foundation model that needs no fitting and forecasts related series together), and a LightGBM model trained across all series at once. Series with seven or more years of history are served as a weighted blend of the two models that did best on a validation year, with the more accurate one counting for more; this is roughly four series in ten. Shorter series, the majority, are served by Chronos-2 alone, because there is not enough history to pick between models fairly. Accuracy is then measured on a further twelve months that played no part in the choice. Across all served series the typical (median) test error is around 8–10%, and every forecast is refitted each month when new data arrives.

The forecasts you see are pre-computed from this pipeline, not generated on the spot. Each one carries the model that produced it, its measured test error, and a confidence label, so the number comes with a sense of how much to trust it.
