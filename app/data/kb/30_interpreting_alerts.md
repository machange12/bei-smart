# How to Interpret an Alert

An AgriPulse alert marked SPIKE means one specific thing: the latest price for that commodity in that market sits outside the normal range of that same series' own history. It does not mean the price is high by national standards, and it does not mean something has gone wrong — it is simply a statistical flag on that one series.

The opposite flag, CRASH, means the latest price sits well below that series' normal range. AgriPulse monitors several hundred series (around 780), and in a typical month only a few dozen carry a flag — mostly SPIKEs, with a handful of CRASHes; the rest are NORMAL. The Alerts page and the Map always show the current list, refreshed monthly.

A NORMAL status is not the same as "stable." AgriPulse separately tracks volatility — how much a series moves over a rolling twelve-month window — and a series can be NORMAL right now while still trending sharply upward or downward over recent months. Reading an alert alongside its volatility trend gives a fuller picture than either on its own: a SPIKE with a rising volatility trend is more concerning than a SPIKE that appears in an otherwise stable series and may simply be a temporary blip.
