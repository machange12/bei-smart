# Policy Events and Shocks That Moved Kenyan Food Prices

Some price moves in the AgriPulse history were caused by a government decision or a one-off shock, not by the season. The models cannot foresee these, but they explain many jumps and drops visible in the charts. Dates below come from press and official reports; where sources disagree on the exact day, the month is given.

**2017: drought, maize shortage and the first flour subsidy.** After a poor harvest, maize flour reached roughly KES 140 to 160 for a 2 kg packet. From mid-May 2017 the government capped subsidised flour at KES 90 per 2 kg and opened a duty-free maize import window. The subsidy ran until late 2017. Maize and maize flour prices fall sharply in mid-2017 for this reason, then recover.

**2018: VAT on fuel.** Fuel became subject to VAT in September 2018, at a reduced rate of 8% after a public outcry over the planned 16%. Fuel feeds into transport, so it raises the cost of moving food between markets.

**2020: COVID-19.** Movement restrictions and curfews from March 2020 disrupted transport and market days. Some series have gaps or erratic readings in 2020 for this reason.

**March 2021: maize import ban.** On 5 March 2021 Kenya banned maize imports from Uganda and Tanzania, citing aflatoxin. The ban was lifted within days on paper, with strict testing conditions, though traders reported delays at the border for weeks afterwards. Western Kenya markets depend on this cross-border maize.

**2020 to 2023: prolonged drought.** Several failed rainy seasons in a row cut harvests and pasture, pushing up maize, beans and livestock-related prices, most severely in the arid north and east.

**2022: global price shock and the second flour subsidy.** The war in Ukraine from February 2022 lifted world grain, fuel and fertiliser prices. Maize flour passed KES 200 per 2 kg. On 20 July 2022 the government capped it at KES 100 per 2 kg; the programme ended around 19 to 20 August 2022 and prices returned to about KES 190 to 215.

**2023: duty-free imports.** To cover the gap before the harvest, the Treasury allowed duty-free imports of white maize and rice from 1 February to 6 August 2023, with more volume added in March 2023. Imports of this kind hold prices down in the months they arrive.

**2023 onwards: fertiliser subsidy.** Subsidised fertiliser was sold at KES 3,500 per 50 kg bag in early 2023, cut to KES 2,500 in mid-2023 and later to KES 2,000, against roughly KES 7,000 or more on the open market in 2022. Cheaper fertiliser supports larger harvests, which tends to ease prices one season later.

**July 2023: fuel VAT doubled.** The Finance Act 2023 raised VAT on petrol, diesel and kerosene from 8% to 16% from 1 July 2023, raising transport costs.

**2023 to 2024: weaker shilling, then El Niño rains.** The shilling lost value through 2023, making imported wheat, rice and cooking oil dearer, and recovered in early 2024. Heavy El Niño rains in late 2023 and floods in March to May 2024 damaged roads and crops in some counties.

When a price in AgriPulse moves sharply around one of these dates, the event is the likely cause. An alert during such a period is real, but it reflects policy or a shock rather than ordinary market behaviour.
