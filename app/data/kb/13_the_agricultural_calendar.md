# Kenya's Agricultural Calendar

AgriPulse's classifier recognises four seasons: Long Rains, Short Rains, Dry Season, and Cool Dry. These are not arbitrary labels — they track the rhythm that drives Kenyan food prices through the year.

The long rains, roughly March to May, feed the main harvest for maize and other staples in most of the country. The short rains, roughly October to December, support a second, smaller harvest, especially important in areas with a bimodal rainfall pattern. Between these windows, dry periods put pressure on stored supply until the next harvest arrives.

This calendar matters for forecasting because the effect of rainfall on price is not immediate. Rain that falls during planting does not affect prices until the resulting crop reaches market months later, which is why rainfall was tested as a model input lagged three and six months, matching the planting-to-harvest cycle. Understanding this calendar is also the simplest way to sanity-check a forecast: prices easing after a good long-rains harvest, or tightening before the short rains land, is expected behaviour, not an anomaly.
