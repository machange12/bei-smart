# Why Prices Vary So Much by Region

Regional price differences in Kenya are real and statistically confirmed, not noise or coincidence. A Kruskal-Wallis test on maize prices from 2020 onward, comparing across regions, returned H = 114.6 with p < 0.001 — a result that would arise by chance in far fewer than one in a thousand samples.

North Eastern carries the highest median maize price in the dataset, at 66.47 KES per kilogram, against Coast at the low end at 36.32 KES per kilogram. This gap tracks transport distance and arid-climate production constraints, not market manipulation: North Eastern counties are far from production areas and depend heavily on maize brought in from elsewhere, while Coast and the production regions of Rift Valley and Nyanza sit closer to supply.

This is why AgriPulse's price classifier defines "cheap," "average," and "expensive" relative to each market's own regional average rather than a single national figure. A price that is entirely normal in Nairobi could be a genuine spike in Kisumu, and treating them the same way would mislabel both.
