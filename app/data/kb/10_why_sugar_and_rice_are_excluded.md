# Why Sugar, Wheat Flour and Rice Are Not Forecast

Sugar, wheat flour, and rice each have around 650 price records in the underlying dataset — on the surface, plenty of data. But every one of those series with more than 40 observations came from a refugee settlement market: Kakuma, Dadaab, or Kalobeyei.

That matters because prices in those settlements are set largely by humanitarian distribution cycles, not by ordinary buying and selling. A forecasting model trained on that kind of series is not learning about a market; it is learning about a distribution schedule, and the result would not transfer to how these commodities actually trade in the rest of Kenya. No mainstream Kenyan market had continuous public price coverage for sugar, wheat flour, or rice in any of the sources used.

This is a different exclusion from potatoes, which were tested in real markets and failed on accuracy. Sugar, wheat flour, and rice were never testable in a mainstream market to begin with — the data needed to try simply is not there.
