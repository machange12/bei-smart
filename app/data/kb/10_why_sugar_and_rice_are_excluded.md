# Why Sugar Is Not Forecast, and How Rice Got In

Sugar, wheat flour, and rice were long excluded. Each had around 650 price records in the original dataset — on the surface, plenty of data. But every one of those series with more than 40 observations came from a refugee settlement market: Kakuma, Dadaab, or Kalobeyei.

That matters because prices in those settlements are set largely by humanitarian distribution cycles, not by ordinary buying and selling. A forecasting model trained on that kind of series is not learning about a market; it is learning about a distribution schedule, and the result would not transfer to how these commodities actually trade in the rest of Kenya. At the time, no mainstream Kenyan market had continuous public price coverage for any of the three. That changed for rice once KAMIS became a major source: it reports rice regularly in ordinary markets such as Ahero, Chuka, Kagio and Ngurubani (Mwea), so rice is now forecast there, and wheat flour has a single series in Nairobi. Sugar still lacks a mainstream series that is long, continuous and current enough, and settlement markets remain excluded altogether.

This is a different exclusion from potatoes, which were tested in real markets and failed on accuracy. Sugar, wheat flour, and rice were originally never testable in a mainstream market to begin with; for sugar, the data needed to try is still not there.
