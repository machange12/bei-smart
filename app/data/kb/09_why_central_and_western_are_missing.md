# Why Central and Western Kenya Were Once Missing

Central and Western Kenya are now covered by AgriPulse, but they were absent for most of this system's development, and the reason is worth understanding because it explains a real pattern in how Kenyan food price data gets collected.

AgriPulse originally combined WFP, FEWS NET, and KAMIS data, but the WFP series that formed the backbone of the pipeline barely touched Central or Western Kenya. Price monitoring in Kenya tends to concentrate on food-insecure areas — places where price shocks translate directly into hunger risk, and where humanitarian agencies need early warning. Central and Western Kenya are both net food-producing regions: they grow more than they need and are comparatively food-secure, so they were historically not the priority for that kind of monitoring infrastructure.

The gap closed when AgriPulse rebuilt its dataset on FEWS NET as the primary source. FEWS NET's own market price collection is far broader than the WFP subset used before — it covers 111 Kenyan markets nationwide, including regular reporting from Central markets like Thika and Kerugoya and Western markets like Butere and Mumias. Switching to FEWS as the primary source didn't add new monitoring effort in Central and Western Kenya; it simply meant AgriPulse started drawing on monitoring that already existed but hadn't been part of the pipeline.
