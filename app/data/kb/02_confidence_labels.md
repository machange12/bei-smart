# What the Confidence Label Means

Every forecast in AgriPulse carries a confidence label: high, medium, or low. This is not a guess about how the market feels — it is set directly from each series' measured error on a twelve-month test period the model never saw during selection.

High confidence means a test error under 10% — the steadiest bean and maize series test as low as 1–2%. Medium confidence means a test error between 10% and 20%. Low confidence means above 20%, up to the 25% cut-off beyond which a series is not served at all. Roughly half of all live series are high confidence and only a small minority are low.

The label tells you how far to trust the number, not whether the model is "good" in some absolute sense. A low-confidence forecast for a noisy market is still the best available estimate for that series — it simply comes with a wider margin for error than a high-confidence forecast for, say, maize in Kitui. Treat high-confidence forecasts as reliable planning input, medium as a reasonable guide, and low as a rough direction rather than a number to act on precisely.
