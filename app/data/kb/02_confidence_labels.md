# What the Confidence Label Means

Every forecast in AgriPulse carries a confidence label: high, medium, or low. This is not a guess about how the market feels — it is set directly from each series' measured error on a twelve-month test period the model never saw during selection.

In the current system, series labelled high confidence had a test error under 10% (as low as 2.2% for Maize in Makueni). Medium-confidence series tested between roughly 11% and 15%. Low-confidence series tested above 20%, up to 29% for Sorghum in Nairobi.

The label tells you how far to trust the number, not whether the model is "good" in some absolute sense. A low-confidence forecast for a noisy market like Nairobi sorghum is still the best available estimate for that series — it simply comes with a wider margin for error than a high-confidence forecast for, say, maize in Kitui. Treat high-confidence forecasts as reliable planning input, medium as a reasonable guide, and low as a rough direction rather than a number to act on precisely.
